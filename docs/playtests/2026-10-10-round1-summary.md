# Playtest round 1: summary of findings

Date: 2026-10-10

Source: fresh players' sessions, relayed by the developer as a summary. There are no per-player timed records for this round, so the M13 exit criterion (two of three players reach the §14 moment in under an hour unaided) is still open.

## What players said

- They enjoyed the game.
- The content felt thin: not enough variety in what there is to build.
- Too few people ever became able to build a real settlement.

## What the headless runs showed

On seed 11, with a chest placed and a cottage offered, and no other help:

- The settlement by the tree reached 14 people by day 24, but building stopped at four cottages with five people homeless for the rest of the run. The cottage needs about 20 stone, the three outcrops near the tree hold about 40, and nobody knew of the quarries on the ridge 70 blocks away, so every later site waited for stone that never came while people relaxed during working hours.
- Once building was unblocked, food became the limit: berries were almost never stored, food in the chests stayed at zero, and in the first winter (days 31 to 40) nine of 27 people starved.
- There was nothing to build beyond homes, a storehouse and an altar, and nothing marked progress toward a settlement that stands on its own.

## Changes made (M13, village track)

- Builders without stone raise plain stone walling in planks.
- New blocks with effects: table and bench (a hall: faster recreation), planter (a garden: faster regrowth, bears in winter, tended by people), lantern (wolves avoid the light, full sight at night).
- Valley designs that migrants bring: timber cottage, store shed, village hall, garden patch. A function reference in the design library.
- Food: gardens are picked into the chests; food work pulls harder while the chests hold under a day of food; people want more put by in autumn; a hungry settlement draws no migrants.
- Settlement standing (camp, hamlet, village) with a checklist in the settlement panel and guide lines that point at what the place lacks.
- A village can be asked to found a second settlement on one of three founding grounds; the settlers carry wood and food, set down a chest and build their own homes.
- Fixes found along the way: people walled in by quarrying clamber out; the unhappy no longer move between settlements every day; settlers sleep and pray near their own camp; nobody waits all day under a tree for shelter instead of building a roof.
